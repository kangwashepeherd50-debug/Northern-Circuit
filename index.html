A Land of History & Mystery

Before Livingstone, the North was the heart of the Bemba Kingdom - Zambia's largest kingdom, ruled from Mungwi since the 1400s. Arab-Swahili traders crossed Lake Tanganyika for ivory, leaving Swahili words still used today.

In 1873, Dr. David Livingstone died in Chief Chitambo's village (Central Province). His heart was buried under a Mvula tree - now the Livingstone Memorial, a sacred site you can visit.

The British left Shiwa Ngandu - Africa's famous English manor built in the bush by Sir Stewart Gore-Browne in 1914 - and Mwela Rock Paintings with 700+ ancient arts, older than the pyramids.

For decades the North was forgotten. Its waterfalls like Kalambo (221m, 250,000 years old - older than Victoria Falls) and parks stayed wild. Now we reopen it.
